# Aloria client payments

Reviewed September 4, 2026. Start with **Square Free invoices** after personally reviewing each website inquiry. The public contact form should continue to collect project information only; it should never collect card or bank details.

## Recommended client flow

1. Review the inquiry and reply from `mia@aloriadesign.com`.
2. Confirm the service, scope, timing, revisions, and deliverables in writing.
3. Send the client a Square estimate and service agreement.
4. After approval, send a hosted Square invoice. The client pays on Square’s secure page and receives a receipt.
5. Begin work only after the required payment has cleared.

For a consultation or a small single deliverable, collect 100% before the appointment or work begins. For a larger project, use 50% when the agreement is signed, 30% after concept approval, and 20% before releasing final files. Square Free can handle these as separate invoices. Square Plus can place milestone payments on one invoice and require a contract signature before payment.

At launch, let clients purchase furniture directly from retailers. If Aloria later purchases and resells products, collect the merchandise, delivery, and applicable tax in full before placing orders.

## Why start with Square Free

Square Free has no monthly subscription and combines estimates, hosted invoices, reminders, contracts, e-signatures, and project records. Clients can pay invoices by card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, or ACH. Card payments are convenient for consultations; offering ACH on larger invoices reduces processing cost.

Upgrade to Square Plus when its consolidated milestone workflow is valuable or monthly processing savings exceed its subscription cost. Current pricing and features should be checked before changing plans: [Square Invoices pricing](https://squareup.com/us/en/invoices/pricing), [invoice payment experience](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/5138-pay-invoices-with-square), [deposits and payment schedules](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/6581-request-deposits-with-square-invoices), and [Square Contracts](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/8133-create-and-send-square-contracts).

## Agreement and recordkeeping

Every project agreement should state the scope, deliverables, revision limits, timing, payment milestones, cancellation terms, refund treatment, and what happens when a client delays feedback. Keep the signed agreement, accepted estimate, invoices, approval emails, change orders, and proof that each deliverable was sent.

Process refunds through the original payment and confirm them by email. Square states that its original processing fee is not returned when a payment is refunded. Keep strong written records in case a client disputes a payment: [Square refunds](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/policy-and-pricing-updates) and [payment disputes](https://squareup.com/help/us/en/article/3882-payment-disputes-walkthrough).

## Florida sales tax boundary

Florida’s published interior-design guidance says design advice by itself is generally not taxable when Aloria does not sell tangible property. If Aloria later sells furniture or décor, the product and related design or installation charges can become taxable, including applicable county surtax. Keep client purchases retailer-direct at launch, and consult a Florida CPA before reselling furnishings: [Florida interior designer tax guide](https://floridarevenue.com/Forms_library/current/brochure/gt800052.pdf).
